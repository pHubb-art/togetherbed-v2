    How it works

    What you are teaching

    Sleep Fit turns a sleeper's answers into a bed. Behind it sits a table of 99 rules. 61 are already built, with numbers one developer tuned by hand. 38 are proposed and have no weight yet. This game asks the whole team about every one of them.

    The questions

    Meet a sleeper. Most questions show you one person. Everything about them is average except one thing, like sleeping on the side or waking with a sore shoulder. You say what that one thing should do to one part of the bed: softer, firmer, or the same. If you pick softer or firmer, you say by how much. You do not see the draft number until after you answer, so your gut is not steered.

    Should it steer the bed. For preferences like sleeping hot or wanting natural materials. You say whether the answer should count, and how hard.

    Keep it or drop it. For fixed rules with no number, like an allergy ruling out latex.

    Turn it down or up. For a handful of technical numbers under the hood that do not fit a sleeper story.

    How answers move the table

    Every softer or firmer answer is worth a number of feel steps. One step is the jump from Medium to Plush. The team weight for a rule is the average of everyone's answers, turned back into the same unit the rule table uses. "The same" counts as zero, and an answer in the opposite direction pulls the average down.

    For proposed rules the team weight is the average strength from 0 (ignore it) to 3 (it outranks the body answers), which is the scale the rule table asks for.

    Nothing here changes the live site. It fills the team weight and team notes columns of the rule table, which you download from the Team page as Excel or CSV with the same columns and rule IDs.

    Why your questions differ from your neighbor's

    The game looks at what the team has answered so far and picks your next question from what it still needs. Rules nobody has touched come first, then rules with only one or two answers, then rules where the team is split. Rules that are already validated show up rarely, mostly as warm-ups. Your first three questions confirm something the team already agrees on.

    Answered, then validated

    The progress bar has two fills. The light one is rules with at least one answer. The dark one follows behind it: rules that are validated, meaning at least three people answered and a clear majority, six in ten or more, said the same thing. If three or more people answered and there is no majority, the rule is Split and the game keeps bringing it back until one side wins. When the dark fill reaches the end, the whole table is validated.

    Points

    10 points for every answer. 15 if you are the first to answer a rule. 12 if you are the second or third, because those are the answers that validate it. 2 more for leaving a note.

    What this is not

    This is the team's judgment, pooled. It has not been tested on real sleepers or pressure data, so treat the result as a better starting point, not as proof.
